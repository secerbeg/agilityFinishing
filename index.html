Balance, Quick Feet & Hip Control

Complete the first 10 activities in the exact timestamp order shown below. Keep the early repetitions controlled; the progression is to increase speed without losing posture, balance or clean foot placement.

01 · 11.06.25 AM

Balance Foundation

What it trains: Control the center of mass while the feet work around the cone. The priority is staying stable instead of letting the upper body sway.

Cue: Stay tall through the trunk, knees soft, feet light.
Progression: Start controlled. Then increase foot speed while keeping the same posture. Final progression: add a quick 3–5 yard exit.
02 · 11.09.05 AM

Lateral Quick Feet

What it trains: Fast side-to-side foot contacts while the body remains balanced and ready to change direction.

Cue: Small steps, hips underneath you, do not cross the feet unnecessarily.
Progression: Increase tempo, then add a coach point/call so the player exits left or right on reaction.
03 · 11.09.58 AM

Single-Leg Control

What it trains: Brief single-leg stability during rapid movement. This helps the player control the body before pushing into the next step.

Cue: Own the landing before the next movement.
Progression: Pause for one count on the support leg, then remove the pause and perform the same action at game speed.
04 · 11.10.59 AM

Change of Direction Balance

What it trains: Lowering the body and reorganizing the feet around a cone before accelerating into the next direction.

Cue: Lower the hips, plant under the body, then push away.
Progression: Begin with a planned direction. Progress to a visual cue so the player must react and cut.
05 · 11.12.05 AM

Feet + Ball Coordination

What it trains: Links foot speed with ball awareness so the player can organize the feet without losing control of the ball.

Cue: Quick feet, soft touch, eyes come up.
Progression: Add a touch on the ball after every movement pattern, then exit into a short dribble.
06 · 11.13.52 AM

Hip Rotation + Exit

What it trains: Rotating the hips while staying balanced enough to redirect the body and accelerate.

Cue: Turn the hips, not just the shoulders.
Progression: Increase the angle of the turn, then add a 5-yard acceleration immediately after the rotation.
07 · 11.15.18 AM

Low Balance → Acceleration

What it trains: Maintaining balance in a lower position before pushing strongly into the first acceleration steps.

Cue: Stay low through the change, then drive out.
Progression: Add a ball at the exit or finish with a short burst onto a pass.
08 · 11.16.32 AM

Ball Control Under Movement

What it trains: Keeping the body organized around the ball while changing speed and direction.

Cue: Move your body with the ball—do not reach from behind it.
Progression: Use the weaker foot, then add a defender/cone that determines which side the player exits.
09 · 11.19.17 AM

Reactive Quick Feet

What it trains: Fast repositioning of the feet with a strong base so the player remains ready for the next action.

Cue: Feet fast, body quiet.
Progression: Add a clap, color, or directional call that determines the final exit.
10 · 11.20.50 AM

Balance → Ball Transition

What it trains: Transfers the balance and footwork work into a soccer action with the ball.

Cue: Control the body first, then take the ball into space.
Progression: Finish with a body feint, accelerate past a cone, or receive a pass immediately after the balance action.

Body Feint Mechanics

The feint works because the defender reacts to the body. The player should attack the defender under control, sell one direction, then take the ball into the opposite space and accelerate.

1. Approach
Attack under control
2. Sell
Shoulder + hip movement
3. Shift Defender
Make them move first
4. Exit Touch
Ball into opposite space
5. Accelerate
First 2–3 steps create separation
Approach distanceDo not perform the move too early. Get close enough that the defender has to respect both directions.
Use the whole bodyThe shoulders, hips and eyes sell the fake. A foot movement by itself is easier to read.
Let defender move firstThe best moment to exit is when the defender shifts weight toward the fake.
Exit with purposeThe first touch after the feint should create space for the sprint or the shot.
Main cue: Sell → shift the defender → touch away → accelerate.
3

Body Feint → Finish

The final clip connects the movement session to the soccer outcome: beat the defender with the body feint, create separation and finish without rushing the shot.

Finishing Sequence

  • Attack the defender: approach with enough speed to create pressure, but stay under control.
  • Sell the feint: shoulder and hip movement should suggest the wrong direction.
  • Exit into the shooting lane: the touch after the feint creates the angle.
  • Head up: once separation is created, see goalkeeper and target.
  • Compose the finish: do not let the speed of the move rush the strike.
Cue: Beat the player → regain control → finish.
Progression: Begin against a cone/passive defender. Then add an active defender, change the starting angle, and require finishes with either foot.

Session Quality Rules

Quality before speedIf balance or foot placement breaks down, slow the activity down.
Both directionsRepeat movements and the body feint from both sides.
Explosive exitsThe movement only transfers to soccer if the player accelerates after creating the advantage.
Finish composedSeparation gives time. Use it—head up, select the target, then strike.
